⚙️ Motor & Bearing Science

From the steel race to the vibration impulse — the complete physics.

🔩 Bearing Structure — Click to Explore
Outer Race
The outer race is a hardened steel ring pressed into the bearing housing. It remains stationary during operation. Defects on the outer race produce highly periodic vibration impulses at a fixed rate (BPFO) because the defect location does not move relative to the load zone. This makes outer race faults among the easiest to detect.
⚠️ Bearing Failure Causes
🔄
Rolling Fatigue
Millions of load cycles create sub-surface micro-cracks → spalling. The dominant cause under normal conditions.
💧
Inadequate Lubrication
No lubricant film → metal-to-metal contact → heat + accelerated wear. Accounts for ~36% of premature failures.
📐
Misalignment
Angular or parallel shaft misalignment concentrates stress on specific rolling elements, initiating localised fatigue.
🌫️
Contamination
Dust, metal particles, or moisture enter lubricant → abrasive scoring of raceway surfaces.
🌊
Corrosion
Moisture attacks hardened steel → pitting → stress raisers for fatigue crack initiation.
⚡
Overload
Forces exceeding design rating cause permanent brinelling — flat spots on raceways from ball indentation.

Why EDM? Electro-Discharge Machining is a non-contact process that removes material via controlled electrical sparks in dielectric fluid. It creates defects of precisely controlled diameter and depth without introducing mechanical stresses in surrounding material — ensuring the defect geometry is reproducible across test specimens, which is essential for a benchmark dataset.
📈 How Defects Generate Vibration — Fault-by-Fault
BALL FAULT
A pit on the ball surface contacts both raceways alternately as the ball orbits AND spins. This creates a double-frequency impact pattern modulated by the ball's orbital speed. Result: complex, harder-to-detect signature — especially at low severity.
INNER RACE FAULT
The inner race rotates with the shaft. A crack passes through the load zone once per revolution. Each ball-over-crack impact is amplitude-modulated by shaft speed — the defect enters and exits the maximum load zone continuously, varying impact magnitude. Detected at BPFI.
OUTER RACE FAULT
The outer race is stationary. Impacts occur every time a ball passes the fixed defect location. Since the defect does not move in or out of the load zone, there is no amplitude modulation — producing the cleanest, most periodic impulse sequence. Detected at BPFO.

📡 Signals & Sensors

What the accelerometer actually measures — and what each waveform is telling you.

🌊 Vibration Waveform — Compare Conditions
📡 How the Accelerometer Works
A piezoelectric accelerometer contains a crystal that generates an electric charge proportional to the mechanical force applied to it. When the motor housing vibrates, the crystal deforms, producing a voltage proportional to acceleration. An ADC samples this at 48,000 times per second, producing 48,000 g-values per second — that's your raw signal.
📍 3 Sensor Positions
DE — Drive End ← YOUR CSV
Nearest to the motor shaft output. Maximum sensitivity to shaft-induced bearing faults. The X097_DE_time channel. This is the signal used to compute the 9 features in your Bearing.csv.
FE — Fan End
Cooling fan side of motor. Captures transmitted vibration from the non-drive bearing. X097_FE_time in the .mat files.
BA — Base
Mounted on motor frame. Captures structure-borne vibration. X097_BA_time. Least direct but captures overall machine health.
💡 Your downloaded dataset uses the Drive End (DE) channel. The feature_time_48k_2048_load_1.csv was computed from X###_DE_time variables extracted from each .mat file.
🧮 The 9 Features — What They Actually Measure
2,048 pts → 9 numbers
RMS
√(Σx²/N)
Overall vibration energy. Rises monotonically with fault severity. Good severity indicator but poor fault-type discriminator.
KURTOSIS
μ₄/σ⁴ − 3
Measures impulsiveness. Near 0 for Gaussian noise. Shoots up when bearing defects create sharp periodic impulses. The most sensitive early-fault indicator.
CREST FACTOR
x_peak / x_rms
Ratio of peak to RMS. Healthy: ~2.5–3.5. Faulted: >5. Detects isolated shock events even when overall energy is still low.
SKEWNESS
μ₃/σ³
Signal asymmetry. Zero for symmetric noise. Inner race faults create directional loading that breaks symmetry — unique skewness signature per fault type.
STD DEV
√(Σ(x−μ)²/N)
Amplitude spread. Highly correlated with RMS (r≈1.0). Provides redundant but stable energy measurement.
FORM FACTOR
x_rms / |x_mean|
Waveform shape ratio. Sensitive to fault type — different bearing fault types produce different waveform morphologies.
MAX
max{x(n)}
Largest positive peak. Single-impulse events from severe faults produce extreme max values far above background noise.
MIN
min{x(n)}
Largest negative peak. Complements max — together they define the amplitude envelope of the vibration window.
MEAN
(1/N)Σx(n)
DC offset of signal. Should be ≈ 0 for pure AC vibration. Gravity components or mounting orientation can create non-zero means.
